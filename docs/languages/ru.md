# Russian in Casus

Built overnight 2026-10-19.

## What the reader does
- **Stress supplied.** Ordinary text marks no stress and writes е for ё. Words are looked up without either and the
  dictionary's stressed spellings are shown over the block (кни́гу), as for Greek accents; Latin letters carry the
  stress too (knígu).
- **Six cases** on the six hues (nominative, genitive, dative, accusative, instrumental, prepositional), plural on a
  dashed border. Prepositions give their cases (в, на: accusative or prepositional; с: genitive, instrumental…) and
  settle on the one their object allows; adjectives and determiners agree with their noun inside the clause.
- **Clause rules** (shared with Latin and Greek): the verb's number rules out nominatives of the other number; words
  joined by и, или share their case; a word that is certainly nominative is the clause's subject, so the other nouns
  are not (Мама пишет письмо: письмо is the object); a caseless dictionary reading (the letter я) loses to cased ones.
- **Verbs** show person, number, tense and aspect; the dictionary form names the aspect partner (чита́ть, impf.; pf.
  прочита́ть).

## Data
- English Wiktionary via kaikki (CC BY-SA): nouns, adjectives, verbs and adverbs only when a form is among the 50,000
  commonest subtitle words (FrequencyWords, CC BY-SA 4.0) — Wiktionary has 214,000 nouns and 198,000 verbs; closed
  classes in full. 45,800 lemmas. Left out: participle declensions, compound futures, pre-1918 spellings, form-of
  entries, and the other pronouns a personal pronoun's table lists.

## Drills
- Cyrillic: small letters and capitals, by sound or name. Noun, adjective and verb tables with random words of the
  same pattern; frequent words (subtitles); phrases and proverbs.
